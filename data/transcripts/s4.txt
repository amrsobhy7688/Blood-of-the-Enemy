Session 4
Recap: the cavern, Mervel, and Zaren’s proclamation
The session opens with a quick recap of the previous confrontation inside the sea-cavern.
The party had been inside the cavern fighting Mervel Haloval, the Crystallized Siren. The fight escalated because Zaren made a public proclamation in front of her and her followers.
Zaren had invoked the name of Azar / Azaraks / Azrax. The exact true name remains unstable in the table’s memory, but it is clearly tied to the primordial being associated with the return of the Father.
The important effect was immediate: all of Mervel’s followers reacted. They began to believe what Zaren was saying, especially because something was already written or marked on his body that made his claim feel prophetic or recognizable.
During the fight:
* Mervel was not fully in control of the situation.
* Zaren had barely attacked. He was mostly moving while people around him died.
* The rest of the party was fighting.
* Lysandra summoned demons, and the demons began attacking people indiscriminately, including Lysandra herself.
* Caspian had fallen unconscious once, then was restored.
* The last moment from the previous session ended with Alex surrounded by three enemies.
Throughout the fight, the party had also been hearing Mervel’s name inside their heads. It was as if she was speaking or screaming her own name mentally without realizing she was doing it.
Mervel’s scream becomes real
This time, the same voice is no longer only inside their minds.
The party hears the sound physically in the cavern: a massive roar-like scream, loud enough to dominate the space.
Mervel screams.
At the same instant, every piece of glass embedded in the party’s bodies turns ice cold:
* the shard in someone’s leg,
* the shard in someone’s hand,
* the shard in someone’s palm,
* the shard in someone’s ear.
Then seawater begins forcing its way into the cavern from everywhere.
It does not merely enter from the cave mouth. It pours in through the walls and openings, filling the chamber violently. The cavern becomes turbulent and chaotic. The candles are extinguished one after another.
As the water overturns everything, the party only catches fragments:
* a face passing beside someone in the water,
* Mervel’s screaming,
* Caspian striking or hitting something,
* Lysandra struggling somewhere in the chaos,
* bodies and followers being swept around.
Then everything goes completely dark.
The tide recedes
An unknown amount of time passes.
The party wakes inside the cavern. The tide has already withdrawn. The water had entered, drowned the cavern, and then receded again.
The four party members awaken very close to each other.
The cavern is mostly dark, but a few candles higher up survived the flood and still provide dim light. The central pool remains where it was. The cavern has returned to something close to its previous shape.
No one else remains inside the cavern except the party.
The place where Mervel had stood is different.
She is still there, but she is no longer whole.
The party notices:
* Silver survived by clinging to a high ledge above the flood line.
* The construct did not move. It simply remained in place because it is extremely heavy. The party remembers how difficult it was to drag it through Hold Mount.
* The followers and bodies that had been in the cavern are gone or drowned.
* Caspian is still injured, but this seems to be from what happened before the flood, not from the water itself.
Mervel’s broken body
Mervel begins to move.
She braces herself against the wall and tries to pull herself upright.
Her body is still crystalline, as before, but now it is shattered and diminished:
* the crystal formations along her body are cracked;
* her right leg is missing;
* her left arm is missing;
* her left leg is cracked along its length;
* the remaining parts of her body look fractured, unstable, and ruined.
She had previously carried herself with presence and authority. Now she looks like something barely holding together.
The group’s perception also catches something behind her.
Where Mervel had been pressed against the wall, the party sees a symbol exposed on the cracked stone. It appears to be a simple black mark:
* a circle,
* with a line through it,
* and the wall itself cracked through or around the mark.
In front of that wall is a natural stone formation, almost like a small altar, basin, or power-bottle shape. The symbol seems tied to the cavern wall itself rather than painted onto an object.
Mervel turns to Zaren
Mervel looks at Zaren.
She seems to understand something, or at least believe she understands something. Her words come with pain, exhaustion, and awe.
She says something to the effect of:
Mervel:
 “I am not convinced you have proven that you are him. I am not convinced you are the Vessel, or the vessel this being will return through.”
She pauses.
Then she clarifies what she does believe:
Mervel:
 “I am convinced you are one of his prophets. One of those who call for the return.”
From the DM’s explanation, the party understands an important structure in the belief system:
* the primordial being has nine heralds / prophets / announcers;
* there are always nine at a given point;
* each of those nine may have nine followers or subordinate heralds of their own;
* Mervel is not asking to be one of the primordial’s first nine;
* she is asking to become one of Zaren’s nine.
Mervel asks Zaren to let her be one of his nine.
This matters because Mervel is not merely surrendering. She is trying to reframe Zaren’s role in the prophecy and place herself under him within that hierarchy.
Mervel addresses Lysandra
With her remaining hand, Mervel gestures toward Lysandra as well.
She says Lysandra, too, is involved.
The party begins trying to understand what Mervel means, especially because Lysandra had previously arrived in Vastraya as part of a sacrifice connected to Caspian. Lysandra herself does not fully know why she was sent, nor does she understand all the implications of the sacrifice.
The DM clarifies Lysandra’s internal position:
* Lysandra knows she was sent here as a sacrifice because of Caspian.
* She may not be fully convinced Caspian did anything truly wrong.
* She has seen Zaren acting with authority, and many of his useful or kind actions were based on incomplete information.
* But now Zaren has suddenly claimed something enormous: not merely that he knows religious lore, but that he may be connected to a primordial return.
* From Lysandra’s perspective, it is almost like a man abruptly saying, “I am your highest god.”
The situation destabilizes Lysandra’s trust.
Zaren tries to help Mervel
Zaren moves closer to Mervel and offers to try something.
He attempts healing or mending magic on her ruined crystalline body, especially on the missing or damaged leg.
Nothing happens.
The damage does not respond to the attempt.
Mervel insists that they go step by step.
She reaches for material from the ground, ash or dust, and uses it in a ritualistic way.
Mervel’s water ritual
Mervel moves to the central pool.
Before entering, she tells Zaren that the water, the suffocation, and the ashes matter. The wording echoes the religious logic of the cavern and the Children of the Flame language:
Mervel:
 “The water, the suffocation, is only one thing. But it is by the ashes, and with the ashes, that everything reveals.”
She smears the ash across herself.
Then she lowers herself beneath the water.
The party notices something strange: there are no bubbles.
Mervel is not visibly exhaling. She does not appear to be drowning in an ordinary way.
Zaren follows her underwater for a time, but he does not suffer any immediate harm from the water. He holds his breath and waits.
When Zaren goes underwater, he notices an important detail:
* the ash Mervel rubbed onto herself does not wash away;
* it remains on her body under the water;
* she is now described as marked thrice.
The meaning of “marked thrice” is understood by Zaren but not fully explained to everyone else. The DM deliberately withholds the full explanation, mirroring how Zaren has information the rest of the party does not.
Silver touches the water
Silver climbs down from the ledge and approaches the central pool.
He touches the water.
The moment he does, he recoils and takes psychic pain. He grabs his head, especially around the crystal formations in his skull, and retreats.
This confirms the water is not harmless to everyone. It interacts with glass, mind, or resonance in a dangerous way.
The party tries to decide what Mervel is doing
The party debates whether Mervel is:
* committing suicide,
* performing a ritual,
* preserving herself,
* transforming,
* or deliberately entering some state of suspended recovery.
Caspian considers trying to move the ground beneath her with magic to lift her from the pool, but the earth does not respond normally. The magic enters the ground and begins to do something, but the earth itself does not move as expected.
The DM clarifies that Mervel’s scream is still audible in some form: deep, continuous, and painful.
Caspian also tries to mentally contact her or understand her state. The impression he receives is that what Mervel is doing is a form of self-preservation, but not in a healthy or conscious way. It is almost instinctive, as if her body or fractured mind is doing it to survive.
The group turns on Zaren’s hidden knowledge
The party begins realizing that Zaren knows more than he has said.
The original plan had been to enter the cavern and extract information from Mervel. Instead, after Zaren got some information from her, it became clear that Mervel also extracted or recognized information from him.
Caspian’s perspective sharpens:
Zaren had come in claiming to be using a strategy, but the result was that Mervel learned things from him, and the party learned that Zaren was withholding major context.
The party understands that Zaren may have intended to tell them eventually, but not now.
This causes immediate tension.
Lysandra, especially, is shaken. She is not merely hearing strange doctrine. She is realizing that Zaren may be tied to a prophecy, a primordial, and a return that she was never told about.
Alex leaves the cavern
Alex has a very human reaction to the madness around her.
From her perspective:
* one person is claiming divine or primordial significance;
* another person is speaking mentally;
* another has strange powers connected to a ring;
* Silver has crystals in his head;
* the construct is following orders;
* the cavern just drowned everyone;
* Mervel is broken and submerged in ritual water.
Alex decides she has had enough.
She leaves the cavern, heading outside after Silver and the construct.
She wants to get away, breathe, and figure out a way back home or toward something sane.
The DM notes this reaction feels very in character: Alex is overwhelmed and wants her father, her home, and something normal again.
Zaren follows Alex
Zaren eventually turns and follows Alex out of the cavern.
As he leaves, he looks toward Caspian and says, in effect:
Zaren:
 “What now?”
Then, more directly, he asks what they have done to his mind.
This opens the next confrontation: Zaren’s hidden history, the party’s mistrust, and the question of whether the claims about Azaraks, the Father, and the vessel are truth, delusion, prophecy, or manipulation.
Outside the cavern: Alex breaks away
Alex leaves the cavern first, shaken and frustrated.
Silver is already outside, and the construct is following him with heavy, obedient steps. Alex does not immediately return to the party’s discussion. She is overwhelmed by what she has just witnessed and begins moving away from the cavern.
The DM frames Alex’s reaction as natural:
* Zaren has just made a claim that sounds almost divine.
* Mervel treated him as prophet, vessel, or something close to it.
* The party’s original plan to question Mervel collapsed into revelation, drowning, and ritual.
* Alex is not sure what is real anymore.
* She wants something ordinary, like home, family, or a clear path forward.
If Alex stays outside long enough while the others continue talking, she can benefit from a short rest-like pause, because she has effectively removed herself from the immediate pressure.
Zaren follows Alex
Zaren turns away from the remaining party members and follows Alex outside.
The cavern path is difficult terrain, but he catches up enough to speak.
Alex is still thinking about what Mervel said: that she was sent here for a purpose. Mervel’s words have lodged in her mind. She is not sure whether she believes them, but she cannot ignore them.
Zaren looks to her and asks:
Zaren:
 “What did they do to my mind?”
He is asking about the party’s interpretation of what happened. He wants to know what they think has been done to him, or what they believe about his connection to the Father / Azaraks.
The group begins walking rather than standing still in the cavern. The conversation continues while they move.
Zaren’s fear: “Was he saying I am evil by nature?”
Zaren, processing what was said inside, turns toward Lysandra and asks something close to:
Zaren:
 “Was he saying I am evil by nature?”
This is tied to the idea of the primordial or Father being inside him, or Zaren being connected to something ancient and dangerous.
There is a moment of mental communication. Someone speaks to Lysandra inside her mind. Zaren can tell something is happening, but he cannot hear the words. He only knows someone is communicating with her mentally.
The DM clarifies that Zaren recognizes the act of telepathic contact, but not the content.
Alex, still outside, settles on a rock, resting and drying herself after the flood.
The party begins discussing Zaren’s hidden past
The conversation shifts into Zaren’s history and what he has hidden.
Zaren explains that he did not enter the cavern blindly. He had been working with assumptions and partial information. He had told the group they were going to meet people “under the First Flame,” and when they saw the ruins outside the cavern, he knew enough to warn them.
He had addressed himself as:
Zaren:
 “Zaren Dryce, servant of Azaraks.”
This was not random improvisation. It came from knowledge Zaren already possessed.
He explains that, from his perspective, he had revealed enough in the cavern to test Mervel:
* What was she doing alone in the cavern?
* What was she doing with the glass?
* What did she truly believe?
* Would she join them or remain an enemy?
* Could she help them understand what was happening in Vastraya?
But the others push back. From their perspective, Zaren’s “test” gave Mervel access to dangerous information and exposed that he had been hiding critical truths.
Caspian’s concern: Zaren is giving power to others
Caspian understands the danger clearly.
The knowledge Zaren is now sharing is not harmless. If these claims were repeated in Cyriath or within the empire, Zaren’s life would be in danger.
The party realizes that Caspian now has considerable power over Zaren. If Caspian reveals Zaren’s connection to Azaraks, the Father, or the Children of the Flame prophecy, Zaren could be destroyed politically, religiously, or physically.
The DM explicitly frames it:
Zaren is, in a sense, handing Caspian his life.
This matters because the party’s trust is no longer theoretical. Their secrets now place them in one another’s hands.
Zaren’s explanation: his father and the prophets
Zaren says he knows some of this because of his father.
His father was connected to the prophecy and the hidden faith. Zaren says he was speaking to Mervel about being a Vessel or a martyr, but Mervel interpreted him through the framework of the prophets.
Zaren admits he does not fully know everything.
There is a crucial distinction:
* Mervel knows parts of the Children of the Flame prophetic structure.
* Zaren knows other hidden parts from his father and his family.
* The party knows fragments from visions.
* No one has the whole truth.
Zaren says, in essence:
Zaren:
 “Everyone thinks they are clever, each hiding two words in their sleeve. But if we put my two words with your two words, maybe we will understand how this is divided.”
This suggests that the truth has been intentionally fragmented across different people, factions, memories, and prophecies.
The vision recap: gods before the current order
The party begins reconstructing what was seen or learned in previous visions.
They recall a time before the current world order, before the gods as the modern empire understands them.
In that ancient period:
* there were gods ruling the world, as in any pantheon;
* conflict began between those gods and Azaraks / the Father;
* the Father predates those gods;
* the world itself may be made from him, or from something belonging to him;
* the gods’ power is somehow borrowed from the same substance or foundation.
The party recalls Haren’s doctrine from the memory-scene:
* the gods are not necessarily false;
* the Sovereigns may not be entirely wrong;
* but the old understanding of what comes after may be wrong;
* the “test” is coming;
* when the test arrives, faith will be revealed.
The DM notes that the religion known to Lysandra came from after the empire’s established order. Zaren is speaking about something from before the empire’s accepted religious history.
That means his claims are not automatically impossible within the party’s knowledge. They are dangerous because they are outside the official doctrine.
Zaren’s old role: hunting hidden believers
Zaren reminds the group that he once hunted people connected to this faith.
He says he had been involved in finding or arresting the people who preached or believed in the return. The party realizes the contradiction:
* Zaren was once an enforcer against these people.
* Yet he also comes from a family connected to the hidden faith.
* His father may have preserved knowledge Zaren himself only partly understood.
* His own past may be built on suppressed or incomplete truth.
This creates tension around whether Zaren was a victim, collaborator, hunter, believer, or all of these at different points.
Lysandra’s ring heats
While the party argues and tries to understand the revelations, Lysandra’s Ring of Echoes heats suddenly.
She touches it.
The ring becomes extremely hot.
Then Lysandra vanishes into the ring.
The physical ring falls to the ground outside.
Zaren touches it and finds it burning hot, but nothing happens to him. Lysandra is no longer standing with them.
Silver sees this happen and, for the first time anyone has heard from him, says:
Silver:
 “What the fuck?”
This is notable because Silver rarely reacts in that way. Even he is stunned by the absurdity of the moment.
Inside the Ring of Echoes
Lysandra finds herself inside a room within the ring.
The space resembles a living room from her family home. It is not an abstract void. It has a domestic, familiar structure. Doors open onto walls, suggesting the space is sealed or pocketed rather than a full house.
On a table in the center is a handwritten note.
The handwriting is her father’s.
This immediately matters because Lysandra’s father is tied to the strange Vermont inheritance, the unflickering Vermont facet, and the whispered power that has been following her.
The note appears to respond to the recent messages and questions she had been sending through the ring.
The rest of the party remains outside, confused and unable to directly see what is happening inside.
The party waits outside the ring
Outside the ring, the others continue talking.
They discuss the factions within the Children of the Flame and the hidden groups tied to the First Flame. There is confusion because not everyone knew these groups had internal divisions.
One person had assumed the Children of the Flame were a single unified faction. Another clarifies that there are many internal divisions, beliefs, and hidden layers.
The group realizes again that each person knows only part of the picture.
No one has the full religious map.
Silver’s stolen notes from Mervel
Silver produces papers or a notebook he took from the ledge inside the cavern.
These turn out to be extremely valuable.
The papers are Mervel’s writings: fragments, memories, notes, and unstable personal reflections.
They are not a clean journal. They are the writings of someone tortured, fractured, and trying to remember through trauma.
The party begins reading.
A strong investigation or reading of the notes reveals several important things.
Mervel’s captivity and experimentation
Mervel’s notes describe what happened to her when she was being tortured and experimented on.
She recorded:
* things she remembered from her own suffering;
* things guards said nearby;
* scraps of overheard information;
* emotional outbursts;
* revenge fantasies;
* guilt and grief;
* a sense that someone was tracking or watching her.
The person who experimented on her is identified:
Malakai, titled Apothecary Prime.
This is a major revelation.
Malakai is not a minor researcher. He is a high-ranking figure connected to the ruling scientific or sanctified order, powerful enough to be known to Caspian. The title “Apothecary Prime” marks him as a top authority in research, medicine, alchemy, or crystal experimentation.
Caspian understands immediately that this is not a random torturer. This is someone at the level of a council-aligned figure or equivalent high office.
Malakai’s project may not be officially sanctioned
The notes suggest that the experiments may have been Malakai’s own project.
The party does not conclude with certainty that the entire Templar’s Office or central religious authority ordered it. From the overheard comments in Mervel’s notes, it sounds more like Malakai was running or directing the project himself.
This matters because:
* the empire may know;
* or parts of the empire may not know;
* Malakai may be operating under official cover;
* or he may be conducting private research using imperial infrastructure.
The transcript does not resolve this.
Glass trials on people
Mervel’s notes reveal that glass was being tested on living people.
The researchers were trying to understand what the glass could do, how it could be implanted, and what effects it had. They were experimenting to see whether glass taken from one body or one condition could be used on another.
The party connects this to their own shards.
The glass is not just an object. It appears to be an exchange:
* the glass gives the bonded person power;
* the person gives something back to the glass;
* the process is not neutral.
This makes the party uneasy about their own embedded shards.
Someone asks whether they should remove the glass from themselves.
The immediate problem is that the only major source of practical information on bonding, extraction, and what glass does to the body may be Sarn, the very place tied to the experiments.
Sarn and the Dirty Docks
Mervel’s notes mention Sarn, a location in Vastraya where exiles are processed or held.
She writes about escaping from Sarn.
One useful location appears in the notes: the Dirty Docks.
This is described as a dock area where ships came to unload supplies for Sarn. It was apparently less guarded than other parts of the facility.
Mervel escaped through that area because she was believed to be dying or already near death, and the guards were less careful.
The party understands that Sarn has a port or dock access, which means it might be reachable by sea or useful as an infiltration point later.
Mervel’s transformation
The notes suggest that Mervel did not choose what she became.
She was experimented on.
Glass was removed, inserted, tested, and used around her. Acid or other damaging substances were placed on her after extraction or trials, apparently to see what happened or to keep testing her condition.
Over time, her body adapted to the glass.
The crystalline growth worsened and spread until she became the Crystallized Siren the party encountered.
Her writings include pages of rage: what she would do to the people who tortured her if she saw them again.
They also include grief and guilt. Mervel blames herself in places, writing that she allowed it to happen, or that perhaps she should have died.
“Buried / dead and reborn in fire”
On the final page, the party finds a phrase written with the tone of mourning or ritual:
“Buried / dead and reborn in fire.”
The exact form is unclear, but the meaning is tied to death, rebirth, and fire.
The party suspects this may mark the moment Mervel shifted from being an ordinary victim into something tied to the Children of the Flame.
The DM emphasizes that these are personal writings from a broken, unstable person. They are useful, but they are not guaranteed to be clean factual reports.
Lirren, the name with a heart around it
In one of the pages, the party notices a name circled with a heart:
Lirren.
This appears to have been someone Mervel loved.
The party recalls or infers that Lirren was another person who escaped from Sarn.
But Lirren only survived for about a day after escaping.
This may explain part of Mervel’s madness and grief.
It is strongly implied that Lirren’s death, or abandonment, or failed escape, shattered something in her.
Immediate choices: Sarn, the encampment, or Mervel
The party begins debating what to do next.
Distances are clarified:
* From the cavern back to the encampment near Hold Watch / Hold Mount: roughly two days.
* From the cavern to Sarn: roughly five to eight days, depending on marching speed.
* Going back to the cavern later for Mervel would create a detour.
* Bringing Mervel with them would be difficult and dangerous.
The party is low on supplies and hungry. They discuss returning to the encampment first for food and provisions before heading to Sarn.
They also consider whether to take Mervel.
The arguments against taking her:
* she is badly broken;
* she is missing limbs;
* she may not be in control of her psychic scream;
* her powers could hurt the party accidentally;
* the encampment may want her head;
* carrying her would slow them;
* she may be safer or more stable in the water for now.
The arguments for taking her:
* she knows Sarn;
* she escaped from there;
* she may know how to reach the Dirty Docks;
* she may understand the glass better than anyone else nearby;
* leaving her behind risks losing a major source of information.
The party does not fully settle this yet.
Returning to Mervel after the tide
After the party reads Mervel’s notes and debates what to do, someone returns toward the cavern once the tide has withdrawn again.
Mervel is still in the pool.
She has not surfaced.
She is still submerged in the ritual water, motionless or nearly motionless, with the ash still clinging to her body. She does not visibly breathe, but the party already suspects that her crystallized state may have changed her need for breath.
Her cracks and missing limbs have not meaningfully repaired. Whatever the pool is doing, it is not healing her quickly.
When one of the party speaks loudly toward her, they immediately hear the same overwhelming psychic scream and fractured thoughts. The sound is not simply in the ear. It strikes the mind.
A Wisdom save is called.
The psychic backlash injures Caspian and Lysandra. Caspian is already badly hurt and is left barely standing. This confirms that Mervel’s mental field remains dangerous even when she is passive.
Mervel does not respond
The party tries to provoke a response from Mervel.
They mention Lirren, the name found in her notes with a heart around it.
There is no useful answer.
They prod her, speak to her, and try to see if she is still aware. She remains submerged and unresponsive. The impression is not that she is calmly refusing to speak. It is closer to her being locked into the ritual state, either preserving herself or mentally elsewhere.
The party remembers they have seen this sort of fixed, inert state before. When they left her earlier, she had already seemed set in place.
The question of taking Mervel
The party seriously considers removing Mervel from the pool and bringing her along.
Possible reasons to take her:
* She escaped from Sarn.
* She knows the Dirty Docks.
* She may understand glass experimentation better than anyone nearby.
* She may be able to guide them through a route into Sarn.
* She is connected to the Children of the Flame and Zaren’s prophetic claims.
Reasons against taking her:
* She is missing a leg and an arm.
* She is not meaningfully mobile.
* She may emit uncontrolled psychic screams.
* She could injure the party or any camp they bring her to.
* The encampment may want her killed.
* She may not be able to speak or help in her current state.
* Carrying her would slow the group and create a major risk.
Someone suggests using the construct to carry her.
The DM reminds the party that Mervel is not simply a helpless wounded NPC. She is unstable, dangerous, and possibly still emitting dangerous mental resonance without choosing to.
The group leans away from taking her by force.
A dead follower as a possible source
The party wonders if one of Mervel’s dead followers could be useful.
They consider asking the construct to retrieve a corpse from the cavern. The idea is to use magic such as Speak with Dead or some revival-like effect to question one of the followers.
This quickly runs into problems:
* The followers have drowned or died in the repeated tides.
* Most of them are certainly dead.
* The party does not currently have the right spell to question the dead.
* Healing magic would not help a corpse.
* Revivification would require very specific timing and resources.
* No one present seems able to reliably bring back or interrogate the dead.
The idea is noted as potentially useful, but not immediately actionable.
Decision: return to the encampment first
The party finally settles on a route.
They will return to the encampment first, rather than going directly to Sarn.
The reasons are practical:
* They need food and supplies.
* They need rest.
* They are injured and magically depleted.
* Sarn is far away, roughly five to eight days depending on pace.
* The encampment is closer, roughly two days away.
* From the encampment, they can prepare for Sarn more safely.
The party decides not to take Mervel with them for now.
This leaves Mervel in the cavern pool, still submerged, still marked, and still unresolved.
Plans for Sarn and Broken Teeth Peaks
The party discusses possible future routes.
If they return to the encampment, then head toward Sarn, they may be able to pass by or near the Broken Teeth Peaks.
Caspian is interested in whether something or someone in that direction may matter to him personally.
The party notes that the route to Sarn could become more than a simple supply run. It may intersect with other unresolved hooks.
Lysandra begins explaining the ring
As the party travels back from the cavern, Lysandra explains what happened when she vanished into the Ring of Echoes.
She tells them that she did not choose to enter the ring. The ring heated, pulled her in, and placed her inside a sealed living-room-like space modeled after part of her family home.
She explains that she found a note from her father.
The note clarifies that the strange things happening to her are tied to the Vermont family inheritance.
The power is connected to the firstborn of the family. A firstborn Vermont can interact with crystals in a way that draws out more energy.
Lysandra explains that this is related to the cuts she makes on crystals and the way those cuts enhance or release power.
She also connects this to the whispering voice she hears in her mind.
The group pushes her for the actual contents of the letter, not just the parts they already know. They want to know what is new.
Lysandra’s father and the unknown voice
Lysandra admits she does not fully understand the voice.
She knows it is speaking to her, but she does not know its identity.
The party asks whether it is her father.
She is not certain.
She also realizes she may not even have settled or remembered her father’s exact name at the table. The name Andrew Vermont is chosen or reaffirmed as her father’s name.
The group considers whether anyone connected to the First Flame or Vermont family history is known to have powers like hers: strange crystal manipulation, pocket spaces, ring interiors, and whispered guidance.
No clear answer is reached.
Travel: drag marks from the shore
During the first hour of travel away from the cavern, the party notices drag marks.
The marks lead from the shore inland.
They look like something, or someone, was dragged from the water and pulled across the ground.
The party examines them but does not immediately follow deeply. They understand that the marks may indicate another body, creature, or object came from the coast and moved inland.
This is recorded as a clue.
First long rest on the return route
The party eventually makes camp and takes a long rest.
Silver sits nearby.
The construct stands watch, heavy and still.
During the rest, the party notices a figure moving through the camp.
It is one of the Hollows, similar to the dead glass-infested people they saw near the shore.
But this Hollow is fresher.
The earlier shore Hollows looked as if they had been dead for months. This one appears to have died only a few days ago. Its body is not as decayed.
It walks through the camp without attacking.
It steps into the campfire and continues moving, taking damage from the flames but showing no meaningful reaction.
The party recognizes that it is dead, not unconscious or alive in any ordinary way.
Glass growths protrude from its body, like the other Hollows.
Examining the fresh Hollow
The party examines the Hollow.
They confirm:
* It is dead.
* It has glass growing out of it.
